# Marketplaces now profit from everything except matching

In November 2026 the large marketplaces make their margins from advertising, logistics and financial services built on top of matching. Matching itself is being commoditised by AI and hemmed in by regulation. Three company-reported figures show the shift. Amazon's advertising revenue in July–September 2026 was **$19.6bn**, more than twice its North American operating income. Etsy's take rate rose to **26.2%** because of ads, not fees. **Nearly half** of Walmart's US marketplace volume now runs through Walmart's own fulfilment service. Growth has left the mature Western horizontals (low-to-mid single digits organically) for Latin America, South-East Asia, TikTok Shop, live commerce and recommerce, while China's giants spent core-marketplace profits on an instant-delivery subsidy war that regulators then shut down. The biggest competitive shock of 2025–26 came from trade policy, not technology: the end of US de minimis relief (August 2025) and the EU's €3 parcel duty (July 2026) removed much of Temu's and Shein's cost advantage and are pushing them into Amazon-style local warehousing. Regulators are deputising platforms as product inspectors, customs agents and tax collectors; the EU has fined AliExpress **€550m** and Temu **€200m** under the Digital Services Act, while Britain is roughly two years behind on marketplace product-safety duties and low-value import relief. Capital has moved from funding entrants to consolidation (Uber–Delivery Hero, DoorDash–Instacart, eBay–Depop). AI has so far changed discovery more than purchasing: in-chat checkout stumbled, incumbents are blocking outside shopping agents, and the clearest damage is to commoditised freelance marketplaces, where Fiverr's active buyers fell **26%** year on year. All six regularities identified in the history of marketplaces still hold, but one, the law's switch from banning off-market deals ("forestalling") to banning platforms from blocking them ("anti-steering"), is now being tested by AI agents. For anyone building a marketplace, the defensible position is to own the work (fulfilment, payment, guarantees and liability), not the listing.

## Growth has left the mature Western horizontals

### Amazon is now a services and advertising business with a shop attached

Amazon remains the scale anchor, and its growth now comes from monetising the marketplace rather than selling goods. In **Q3 2026 (July–September, reported 29 October)**, net sales were **$201.9bn (+12%)** and operating income was **$28.1bn**, a 13.9% margin that Amazon calls its highest ever. AWS earned $17.8bn of that ([Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q3-2026-report)) (company-reported; high). Online-store sales grew only **1.6%**, because Prime Day moved into Q2 in most large countries; Amazon puts the drag on total growth at about 400 basis points ([TheDesk](https://thedesk.net/2026/10/amazon-q3-2026-earnings-report/); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/ecommerce-marketing/amazon-q3-2026-advertising-revenue-prime-day-q4-guidance/)). Advertising was **$19.6bn, +12% excluding currency effects**, down from +26% in Q2. Third-party seller services grew about **9%**, against 16% in Q2 2026 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-more-than-400m-customers-have-used-alexa-for-shopping-in-the-last-12-months/); [EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-q2-2026-earnings/)). The Q3 seller-services figure is relayed second-hand (moderate). The four quarters of 2025 sum to about **$172bn** of third-party seller services revenue (my calculation from filings, [OpenCapital](https://www.opencapital.sh/stocks/amzn/metrics/amzn_segment_third_party_seller_services_net_sales)). Amazon does not disclose marketplace GMV.

The marketplace's share of Amazon's own volume has stopped rising. Third-party sellers' share of paid units peaked at **62% in Q4 2024**. It was **60% in Q1 2026** and 61% in Q2 2026, as Amazon's first-party retail grew faster ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-is-regaining-1p-unit-share); [EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/amazon-q2-2026-earnings/)) (high).

Behind one storefront sit two seller markets. Chinese sellers made up **62.3% of new Amazon.com registrations in 2024**, against 7.1% in 2015. They hold **55.9% of positions in the top 10,000 sellers**. But US sellers earn **65.3% of that tier's GMV** and **93.2% of the top 100's** ([Marketplace Pulse repost](https://sellersasksellers.com/t/china-reaches-global-majority-on-amazon/6554); [My Amazon Guy citing Marketplace Pulse](https://myamazonguy.com/news/chinese-sellers-on-amazon-take-over-top-10000/)) (analyst estimates relayed second-hand; moderate). The usual claim that "Chinese sellers dominate Amazon" is true by seller count, not by revenue at the top.

### China's giants paid for a delivery war out of core profits, then regulators ended it

China's three big marketplaces are flat or shrinking in their core businesses. In Alibaba's **June 2026 quarter**, customer management revenue (the fees and ad income it earns from merchants) fell **7% as reported**. Excluding a merchant-subsidy programme that Alibaba now books as a deduction from revenue (contra revenue), it rose 1%. Quick-commerce revenue rose **45%** ([Alibaba 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)). Segment definitions changed that quarter, so quarter-on-quarter comparisons do not hold. For the year to March 2026, adjusted EBITA in Alibaba's China e-commerce group fell **44%**, to RMB107.5bn, which Alibaba attributes to quick-commerce and related investment ([Alibaba FY2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001577552/000110465926060224/tm2614494d1_ex99-1.htm)) (company-reported; high).

At JD.com, Q2 2026 revenue fell **2.9%** and its "new businesses", food delivery included, lost **RMB9.9bn** in the quarter ([KrASIA](https://www.kr-asia.com/jd-com-profit-rises-despite-lower-second-quarter-revenue)). PDD's Q2 revenue rose **8%** but net income fell **12%** ([PDD 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001737806/000110465926100534/tm2623874d1_ex99-1.htm)). Meituan guided to a **2025 net loss of RMB23.3–24.3bn**, against a RMB35.8bn profit in 2024 ([Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-02-14/meituan-warns-of-loss-as-high-as-35-billion-amid-subsidy-war-102414811.html)). It returned to an adjusted profit of RMB2.16bn in Q2 2026 as discounting "subsided" ([ESM/Reuters](https://www.esmmagazine.com/technology/meituan-swings-to-profit-in-q2-as-price-wars-subside-320305)).

The war is cooling because the state intervened. The market regulator (SAMR) fined seven platforms **RMB3.6bn** in April 2026 ([Internet Retailing](https://internetretailing.com.au/meituan-posts-another-loss-but-says-food-delivery-wars-are-beginning-to-abate/)), and new platform pricing rules took effect on 10 April 2026 (discussed below). September-quarter results, due later in November, will show whether rationalisation has held.

### Latin America, South-East Asia, social video and secondhand are where growth is

MercadoLibre's GMV rose **42% in dollars** in Q1 2026, and its advertising revenue rose 73% ([MercadoLibre via 01net](https://www.01net.it/mercado-libre-kicks-off-2026-with-fastest-revenue-growth-in-almost-four-years-as-strategic-investments-drive-market-share-gains/)) (company-reported; high). Shopee's Q2 2026 GMV rose **28.4% to $38.3bn**. But its adjusted EBITDA was only $255m, about **0.7% of GMV** (my calculation), so scale has not yet produced Amazon-like margins ([Databoks](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/technology-telecommunications/statistics/6ae852e247c32/shopees-gmv-and-order-volume-hit-new-highs-in-q2-2026); [Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/default/equity/se-adr-on-track-6-month-high-mixed-q2-ceo-bets-shopee-1b-adj-ebitda/cZoZwpiRJQN)).

TikTok Shop's global GMV is estimated at **$50.3bn in H1 2026, up 92%**, with the US its largest market at $11.8bn ([Tubefilter, citing Momentum Works](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/06/tiktok-shop-gross-merchandise-value-gmv-50-billion/)). Every TikTok Shop GMV figure traces to one analyst firm (Momentum Works, with Tabcut) that does not publish its method. ECDB's independent estimate for 2025, €57.1bn, is close to Momentum Works' $64.3bn ([ECDB](https://ecdb.com/blog/tiktok-shop-revenue-development/5262)) (analyst estimates; moderate). A US joint venture took over TikTok's US data and algorithm in January 2026, but ByteDance kept the commercial businesses: e-commerce and advertising ([Asia Tech Review](https://www.asiatechreview.com/p/tiktok-us-deal-looks-like-a-major)) (moderate–high).

In Europe, Allegro's international GMV rose **82%** in Q2 2026 ([Allegro](https://about.allegro.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Press-release_preliminary-Q226-PL.pdf)). Vinted's 2025 GMV rose **47% to €10.8bn** ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/vinted-reaches-1-1-billion-euros-in-revenue-for-2025-accepting-a-strategic-drop-in-profits/2026040971654)).

The incumbents tell a different story. eBay's Q3 2026 GMV rose 15%, but only 13% excluding acquisitions. Its US business grew 27% (22% excluding acquisitions), led by collectibles and trading cards, while international grew 4%. Excluding Depop and Tise, active buyers rose **1%** ([eBay 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065088/000106508826000209/exhibit991erebayq32026.htm)). Zalando's Q2 2026 GMV rose 20.7% as reported but **4.4% pro forma** once About You's consolidation is stripped out ([Zalando](https://corporate.zalando.com/sites/default/files/media-download/Zalando-SE_Q2-2026_Fact-Sheet.pdf)).

### Britain has a high online share, Temu has wide reach and Vinted dominates resale

Online sales were **28.8% of British retail in September 2026**, with online spending up 6.8% year on year against total volumes up 0.8% ([ONS](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/retailindustry/bulletins/retailsales/september2026/pdf)) (official statistic; high).

Ofcom's adult audience panel for May 2025 ranks Amazon first by reach (43.2m adults, 88%) and eBay second (30.7m). **Temu was third at 28.4m, up from 13.8m two years earlier.** Vinted (14.3m) and Shein (13.9m) also made the top ten ([ChannelX, relaying Ofcom](https://channelx.world/2025/12/ofcom-online-nation-report-reveals-uk-top-10-retail-sites/)) (panel estimate; moderate–high).

Shein's UK revenue was **£2.05bn in 2024, up 32%** ([inkl/Guardian](https://www.inkl.com/news/online-fashion-retailer-shein-s-uk-sales-leap-by-a-third-to-more-than-2bn)). Card-panel data put its share of UK apparel spend at **7.6% in Q2 2026**, against 5.0% in the US, but its share has stopped growing and is falling among 18–34s ([Consumer Edge](https://www.consumeredge.com/resources/insight-flash/sheins-uk-growth-stalls-as-young-shoppers-turn-away/)) (moderate).

In C2C fashion the CMA, as relayed in coverage of eBay's results, put Vinted at **50–60% of UK GMV in 2025**, with eBay and Depop losing share ([Value Added Resource](https://valueaddedresource.net/ebay-q3-2026-earnings)) (regulator finding relayed second-hand; moderate). That is the context for eBay's purchase of Depop. A consumer panel counts about **17m UK Vinted customers in 2025**, behind only Primark and Next among fashion retailers ([Retail Week](https://www.retail-week.com/fashion/chart-vinted-now-has-more-customers-in-the-uk-than-all-but-two-fashion-brands/7050502.article)) (moderate).

Four UK gaps matter. Amazon's UK sales were not obtained. Ofcom's 2026 Online Nation report had not been checked, and no figure for UK TikTok Shop GMV was verified.

## Six forces explain most of what has changed since 2024

### Advertising and fulfilment, not commission, now pay the bills

The core economic fact of 2026 is that marketplaces earn their margin above the commission line. Amazon's Q3 2026 advertising revenue of $19.6bn was **more than twice** its North American segment operating income of **$8.7bn**, itself about a 7.7% margin on $112.9bn of sales ([Amazon](https://aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-earnings-q3-2026-report)). Amazon does not disclose advertising margins, so the inference that its retail and logistics business is thin on a fully loaded basis once ads are excluded is mine, not Amazon's (moderate). The same pattern appears down the league table. Etsy's take rate rose from **24.2% in FY2025 to 26.2% in Q3 2026**, which Etsy attributes mainly to Etsy Ads rather than any fee increase ([SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/etsy-q3-2026-results-take-rate-buyers-sellers/)). Shopee's core marketplace revenue (transaction fees plus ads) rose **65.6%** against GMV growth of 28.4% ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/default/equity/se-adr-on-track-6-month-high-mixed-q2-ceo-bets-shopee-1b-adj-ebitda/cZoZwpiRJQN)). Walmart Connect US advertising grew **43%** excluding VIZIO ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/08/20/walmart-releases-q2-fy27-earnings)). MercadoLibre's ads were about **2.6% of GMV** in Q4 2025, against Raymond James's estimate of about **7%** for Amazon ([Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/mercadolibre-gmv-hits-19-8b-as-brazil-grows-36-and-ad-revenue-surges-67)) (analyst estimate; moderate). That gap is the runway every follower marketplace is now chasing.

The runway is shorter than it looks. WARC estimates global retail media at **$200.4bn in 2026**. It forecasts 2027 growth of 11.5%, but only **9.8% excluding Amazon**, the lowest rate it has recorded. It credits Amazon with **78% of US retail media** in 2025 and more than two-thirds in the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Spain ([The Desk, summarising WARC](https://thedesk.net/2026/08/warc-retail-media-ad-market-to-top-200-billion-as-growth-slows/)) (analyst estimate; moderate–high). The implication cuts against the followers. Advertising is the best margin lever in marketplaces, and it is concentrating in the market leader. Marketplaces below the top two will find it harder to grow margins this way (moderate).

Fulfilment is the second engine, and the logistics layer is becoming the marketplace's main point of control. "Nearly 50%" of Walmart's US marketplace business now flows through Walmart Fulfillment Services ([Walmart](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/08/20/walmart-releases-q2-fy27-earnings)). Zalando's B2B arm (the ZEOS fulfilment service and SCAYLE software) grew revenue **27.6%** in Q2 2026 at a 12.2% adjusted EBIT margin, against 5.3% for its own retail business ([Zalando](https://corporate.zalando.com/sites/default/files/media-download/Zalando-SE_Q2-2026_Fact-Sheet.pdf)). Alibaba folded Cainiao's commerce logistics back into its e-commerce group in the June 2026 quarter ([Alibaba 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)), and Vinted Go now runs more than 500,000 pick-up and drop-off points in five markets ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/vinted-reaches-1-1-billion-euros-in-revenue-for-2025-accepting-a-strategic-drop-in-profits/2026040971654)) (all company-reported; high).

For sellers, the cost burden has moved off the headline price list and into an invisible stack. Amazon's referral fee has stayed at about 15% for more than a decade, and its January 2026 fulfilment (FBA) fee rise averaged just **$0.08 per unit**, which Amazon calls "significantly less than inflation" ([Amazon](https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/update-to-u-s-referral-and-fulfillment-by-amazon-fees-for-2026)) (company-reported). The increases arrive elsewhere: a 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge from 17 April 2026 ([AMZ Prep](https://amzprep.com/amazon-fba-fees/)) (vendor; moderate), inventory-discipline fees, and above all advertising. Marketplace Pulse estimated in 2023, from a sample of seller P&Ls, that Amazon took **more than 50%** of sellers' revenue because FBA had become dearer and advertising "not optional" ([Marketplace Pulse](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazon-takes-a-50-cut-of-sellers-revenue)) (independent analysis; moderate). A July 2026 model by a CFO firm that sells to Amazon brands agrees on the range: an all-in deduction of **39.1%** on a $40 item as a floor, rising to about **59%** at $15 because per-unit fees are regressive in price ([Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/amazon-all-in-take-rate)) (vendor model; moderate).

Bill Gurley's 2013 prescription of a low base rake plus optional paid visibility became the industry norm. The optional part has become close to compulsory: Etsy's Offsite Ads are mandatory for shops above $10,000 a year ([beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/09/etsy-2026-fee-hike-ai-disclosure-rules-handmade-sellers-guide)).

Money still flows, but selectively, to profitable leaders and new formats. Vinted sold **€880m of secondary shares at an €8bn valuation** in April 2026, up from about €5bn in 2024, raising no primary capital ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/04/27/vinted-hits-eur8b-valuation-as-eqt-leads-eur880m-secondary-share-sale/)). Whatnot, the live-shopping marketplace, raised at **$20bn** in August 2026, up from $11.5bn in October 2025 ([Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/whatnot-closes-545m-usd-series-g-at-20b-usd-valuation/)). The other side of the ledger is Faire, whose November 2025 employee tender valued it at $5.2bn, about 58% below its 2021 peak ([Faire](https://www.faire.com/news/2025-11-17-a-milestone-for-faire); [Retail TouchPoints](https://retailtouchpoints.com/topics/inventory-merchandising/400-million-funding-round-pushes-wholesale-marketplace-faires-value-to-12-4b)). Vinted's price is about 7 times its 2025 revenue (my calculation). The median public SaaS company traded at 4.6 times revenue in August 2026, after hitting **3.2x in June 2026** in what Aventis calls an "AI disruption sell-off" ([Aventis](https://aventis-advisors.com/saas-valuation-multiples/)). A tentative reading is that investors will pay up for profitable consumer marketplaces with network effects while software is de-rated (low–moderate; no sourced comparison of multiples across listed marketplaces exists).

**UK angle.** British food delivery is now largely in foreign hands: Deliveroo under DoorDash, Just Eat under Prosus, and Uber Eats. UK market shares were not found. Whether competition authorities treat these deals permissively will be tested by the Uber–Delivery Hero and DoorDash–Instacart reviews in 2027–28.

### AI has changed discovery faster than checkout, and incumbents are walling off

The evidence separates cleanly into what has happened and what is predicted.

**What has happened: discovery has shifted and converts well.** Adobe measures more than a trillion visits to US retail sites. In **July 2026**, AI-referred visitors converted **60% better** than other traffic and produced 53% more revenue per visit, reversing a year-earlier position in which non-AI visits were worth 128% more ([Adobe](https://business.adobe.com/assets/pdfs/resources/sdk/ai-traffic-trends-report-august-2026/ai-traffic-trends-report-2026-08-19.pdf)) (measured; high). Adobe does not report AI's share of total retail traffic. Clickstream data put AI tools at only **1.72% of desktop search visits in Q1 2026** ([PPC Land, citing Datos](https://ppc.land/google-still-leads-shopping-searches-as-ai-falls-short-at-7/)) (panel estimate; moderate). Inside the platforms, the strongest causal evidence is a set of randomised field experiments on an unnamed cross-border platform. Generative AI raised sales by between zero and **16.3%** depending on the workflow, through conversion rather than basket size, with no rise in returns ([Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12049)) (independent; high at abstract level).

**What has happened: third-party checkout stumbled.** OpenAI launched Instant Checkout in September 2025. On 24 March 2026 it handed checkout back to merchants "while we focus our efforts on product discovery" ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/what-went-wrong-with-chatgpts-instant-checkout/)) (high). Walmart said items bought inside ChatGPT converted at **one-third the rate** of items requiring a click-through to Walmart.com, across about 200,000 products ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/walmart-chatgpt-checkout-converted-worse-472071)). That is company-reported and comes from one retailer, but it is the only hard conversion figure available.

**What has happened: incumbents are building their own agents and excluding others.** Amazon's assistant, renamed Alexa for Shopping in May 2026, has had **400m+ users in 12 months** ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-more-than-400m-customers-have-used-alexa-for-shopping-in-the-last-12-months/)). Amazon's earlier claim of "nearly $12bn" of incremental annualised sales has no disclosed method and is probably inflated by self-selection, since heavy shoppers use the assistant more (company-reported; treat as directional). Amazon already sells sponsored prompts inside the assistant ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/technology/amazon-says-its-ai-shopping-assistant-is-gaining-traction-with-rufus-users-up-115/)). It blocks ChatGPT's crawlers and Meta's Muse agent, and eBay has reportedly banned autonomous shopping agents ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/alexa-for-shopping-jassy-ai-shopping-agents-meta-muse); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/06/ebay-ceo-jamie-iannone-ai-tools-ai-buyers-shopping-agents-earnings/)) (eBay: single report; moderate).

On the other side, Google, Shopify, Walmart, Visa and Mastercard back open protocols that keep the retailer as merchant of record; Google says its agentic checkout will reach the UK only "later" ([Google](https://blog.google/intl/en-africa/company-news/google-shopping-cart/)). In August 2026 the Ninth Circuit vacated Amazon's injunction against Perplexity's shopping agent. Its reasoning, on the record so far: "It is the user who 'accesses' Amazon's computers, with the help of the Assistant" ([Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/ninth-circuit-lifts-block-on-ai-powered-shopping-assistant/)) (high). The camps line up with market position: dominant closed marketplaces exclude outside agents, while challengers and payment networks push interoperability.

**What is predicted, and where experts disagree.** Experts disagree on whether agents will disintermediate marketplaces. Casey Winters argues that when LLMs "also execute the purchase, the marketplace disappears entirely" ([Winters](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/when-agents-attack-how-ai-collapses)). b2venture argues the reverse: marketplaces supply the "trust API" agents need, because "intelligence can automate the *process* of vetting, but it cannot assume the *liability*" ([b2venture](https://b2venture.vc/stories/part-i---marketplaces-x-ai-the-end-of-marketplaces-as-we-know-them)). ARK expects agents to rank marketplaces on price, inventory and speed, which would favour logistics-heavy leaders ([ARK](https://www.ark-invest.com/articles/analyst-research/are-marketplaces-defensible-in-the-age-of-ai-purchasing-agents)). All three are investor or practitioner essays.

What is **not known** is the share of purchases that agents complete. The only figures are surveys, such as L.E.K.'s April 2026 finding that 8% of AI users had bought something entirely through an agent, and these are relayed second-hand by a tracker and use incompatible definitions ([sanbi.ai](https://sanbi.ai/blog/agentic-commerce-tracker-ai-checkout)) (low). Visa reported "hundreds" of agent-initiated transactions in December 2025 while predicting "millions of consumers" would use agents by holiday 2026 ([Visa](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251218812271/en/Visa-and-Partners-Complete-Secure-AI-Transactions-Setting-the-Stage-for-Mainstream-Adoption-in-2026)).

The online travel agencies give the most revealing evidence: they are paying the new channel. In Q1 2026 Expedia spent **54%** of revenue on marketing and Booking **38%**. Expedia said ChatGPT ads went live in February 2026 and that answer-engine optimisation was its fastest-growing channel ([PhocusWire](https://phocuswire.com/news/finance/ota-marketing-investment-q1-2026)). My reading is that AI assistants are adding new toll collectors to the funnel (sponsored answers, checkout fees) rather than abolishing tolls (moderate).

Amazon's ad slowdown to +11–12% in Q3 is not evidence of agent disintermediation. Management attributes it to timing and guides to "high-teens" growth in Q4.

**UK angle.** Two UK portals are hedging against AI search. Auto Trader has a ChatGPT integration through MCP, the Model Context Protocol that lets AI assistants call outside services ([Auto Trader](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/autotrader-LON:AUTO/news/autotrader-grp-full-year-results-for-the-year-ended-31-march-2026-019e6dd5-0ee5-77c4-8a37-bd3d575579bb/)). Rightmove is building a ChatGPT app ([ii](https://www.ii.co.uk/analysis-commentary/ii-view-rightmove-restarts-share-buybacks-ai-spend-gathers-pace-ii538293)).

### The end of de minimis is pushing Chinese challengers into Amazon's model

The direct-from-factory parcel model behind Temu and Shein rested on customs exemptions, and those exemptions are closing.

**United States.** The US suspended its $800 de minimis exemption for China and Hong Kong in spring 2025; whether the date was 2 May or April is unresolved. It suspended the exemption globally from **29 August 2025**. The statutory repeal takes effect on 1 July 2027 ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-suspend-customs-de-minimis-entry-most-shipments-august-29-2025)). The Supreme Court struck down tariffs imposed under IEEPA, the emergency-powers statute, in February 2026, but the de minimis suspension survived on a separate footing. On 13 August 2026 the Court of International Trade upheld the president's power to rescind de minimis ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/the-800-free-pass-is-gone-cit-confirms-president-can-eliminate-de-minimis-tariff-exemption/)) (high). The effects were quick. Shein's US sales fell **8%** in September 2025, the first full month without the exemption (panel data; [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/shein-us-sales-drop-after-de-minimis-loophole-ends)). Its Hong Kong IPO filing, relayed second-hand, shows US revenue down **14%** in Q1 2026 and a global loss of **$99m** for the quarter, against a $395m profit a year earlier ([Business of Home](https://businessofhome.com/articles/why-american-shoppers-got-tired-of-shein-and-temu)) (moderate). Over the same period Walmart's marketplace grew more than 50% and Amazon's first-party share recovered, which suggests incumbents gained most, though no source measures the transfer of share directly (moderate).

**European Union.** A temporary **€3 duty** has applied to parcels under €150 since **1 July 2026**, running to 1 July 2028. An EU-wide handling fee starts around November 2026; its amount is unconfirmed, with €2 widely trailed ([Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/new-eu-customs-duty-handling-fees-and-vat-requirements-starting-from-2026-five-things-ecommerce-busi)). Sources disagree on how the duty applies: Retail Gazette and EcomCrew describe it as charged per tariff category in each consignment, while Bird & Bird says per item, and only where VAT is settled through IOSS (the EU's import one-stop shop for VAT). The early effects resemble the American ones on a smaller scale. In July 2026, panel data showed Temu's French sales down **38%** month on month and Shein's down 22%, and Sensor Tower put Temu's EU monthly active users down 30% year on year ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/?p=67616)). That is one month of data relayed through a newsletter (low–moderate). PDD's co-CEO said the duties would have "a considerable impact" ([ShopAppy](https://shopappy.com/ecommerce/temu/pdd-q2-2026-results-temu-eu-parcel-duty)), and Temu aims to fulfil **more than 80% of European orders from local warehouses by end-2026** ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/?p=67616)).

**The effect on the challengers' model.** Local warehousing moves cost "from the platform's shipping line to the seller's balance sheet" ([ShopAppy](https://shopappy.com/ecommerce/temu/temu-local-vs-fully-managed)). It also erodes the cost gap with Amazon and Allegro. In Temu's fully managed model the platform already sets prices. ShopAppy's illustration leaves a supplier about $0.60 gross margin on a $7.99 item, and sellers report payment taking 60 days or more (illustrative and anecdotal; low–moderate). The squeeze has not killed the challengers. AliExpress reached operating profit in the June 2026 quarter ([Alibaba 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001577552/000110465926099220/tm2623667d1_ex99-1.htm)), and JD's European marketplace Joybuy doubled its revenue in two quarters ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/jdcom-q2-earnings-call-highlights)). Trade policy is converging the challengers with Amazon's model, not eliminating them (moderate).

**UK angle.** Britain is now the largest Western market where the parcel model still enjoys relief. The £135 low-value import relief stays until at least 31 December 2026. The Autumn Budget 2025 committed to abolish it "by March 2029 at the latest", with liability to be shared between sellers, marketplaces and carriers ([33 Bedford Row](https://33bedfordrow.co.uk/insights/bulletins/the-end-of-the-135-customs-duty-relief-what-importers-and-platforms-need-to-know)). Retail Gazette reports duties "from October 2028" ([Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/07/shein-and-temu-hit-by-new-eu-customs-duty-on-cheap-parcels/)). The two dates may be compatible, and the conflict is unresolved. Shein's higher UK share and Temu's 28m UK adult reach make the UK timetable a swing factor for both (moderate).

### Regulators are deputising platforms, and Britain trails by about two years

The regulatory direction is now consistent across the West. Marketplaces are treated as accountable intermediaries with affirmative duties, not as passive hosts.

**EU product safety through the DSA.** The Digital Services Act has become the EU's main product-safety enforcement tool, and its marketplace cases follow a template: proceedings, preliminary findings, a fine against the risk-assessment process, an action plan, then an audit. AliExpress was fined **€550m on 20 July 2026** after the Commission found that illegal products stayed online for weeks, penalised shops stayed active and its anti-counterfeit system was understaffed ([Obelis](https://www.obelis.net/news/aliexpress-fined-550-million-under-the-dsa/)). Temu's €200m fine of 28 May 2026 was followed by an action plan the Commission accepted on **26 October 2026** ([LexisNexis](https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/news/commission-accepts-temus-action-plan-to-remedy-eu-dsa-risk-assessment-breach-concerning-illegal-products)). Shein has faced formal proceedings since **17 February 2026** ([Euronews](https://euronews.com/next/2026/02/17/eu-opens-formal-investigation-into-shein-over-sale-of-child-like-sex-dolls-and-addictive-a)). Amazon and Zalando both challenged their designation as very large online platforms and lost in the General Court, on 19 November and 3 September 2025 respectively, which gives the regime a firm legal footing pending Zalando's appeal ([eucrim](https://eucrim.eu/news/gc-designation-of-amazon-as-vlop-was-in-line-with-fundamental-rights/)). Every DSA marketplace fine so far has hit a China-linked platform. That reflects the absence of any published finding against Amazon or Zalando, not proof that they comply (moderate).

**EU competition and customs.** The Digital Markets Act review of 28 April 2026 found the regime "fit for purpose" and designated no AI service ([Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/blog/european-commissions-first-review-digital-markets-act-ai-and-cloud-spotlight)). A customs reform provisionally agreed in March 2026 creates an EU Customs Authority and data hub and gives e-commerce operators "increased responsibilities" ([KPMG](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/03/agreement-reform-eu-customs-code.html)). Whether platforms become "deemed importers" is not confirmed.

**Testing shows an industry-wide problem.** Belgian lab tests of 162 products (November 2025) found **73%** of Shein's and about two-thirds of Temu's products non-compliant. Both platforms removed flagged items but not equivalent listings ([Euroconsumers](https://euroconsumers.org/systemic-failures-in-product-compliance-on-temu-and-shein)). In June 2026 Which? tested 15 USB chargers from seven UK marketplaces. **Nine posed a shock risk**, including chargers from Amazon (Haul among them), eBay, B&Q and Debenhams. The Temu and Shein chargers passed the safety tests but lacked UK importer markings ([RTIH](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2026/6/9/phone-chargers-sold-on-amazon-haul-bq-and-ebay-can-electrocute-and-explode-which-warns-shoppers)) (small sample; moderate). Unsafe third-party listings are not a China-only problem. And removing items does not prevent them reappearing, which points to "stay-down" and pre-listing controls as the next regulatory demand.

**Britain.** The UK is behind on marketplace duties specifically. The CMA gained direct consumer-fining powers in April 2025, but its first substantive fine (**£4.2m**, AA Driving School, April 2026) and a **£889,200** fine on StubHub UK (June 2026) both targeted hidden fees and came nowhere near the 10%-of-turnover cap ([Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2026/08/were-just-getting-started-the-cmas-new-consumer-enforcement-powers-in-action)). In March 2026 it opened five fake-review investigations, including into Autotrader and Just Eat ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/the-cma-steps-up-its-fight-against-fake-reviews)); no marketplace has yet been fined. Marketplace product-safety duties under the Product Regulation and Metrology Act are still proposals. The consultation, which ran from **31 March to 23 June 2026**, covered a statutory duty to prevent dangerous listings, know-your-business checks, pre-listing assurance for high-risk goods and a possible UK responsible person for overseas sellers ([HSF Kramer via Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/consumer-protection/from-passive-host-to-proactive-gatekeeper-online-marketplaces-in-the-spotlight-under-the-prma-reforms)), and Which? says the rules "have been delayed". Britain is therefore consulting in 2026 on duties the EU applied in 2024 (moderate). Tax is where deputisation is furthest along. HMRC's seller-reporting rules (more than 30 sales or about €2,000 a year) have applied since January 2024 ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2024/01/04/on-the-eighth-day-of-christmas-hmrc-gave-to-platforms-102iw81)). HMRC has also assessed Uber about **£2.6bn** of VAT for March 2022 to December 2025 over its use of the tour operators' margin scheme, which legislation closed to private-hire operators from 2 January 2026; Uber has paid £1.4bn and is appealing ([Uber 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000041/uber-20260930.htm)) (high).

**United States.** US law is converging on the view that **fulfilment, not listing, creates liability**. The CPSC held Amazon to be a "distributor" of products it fulfils (2024–25), and Amazon is contesting this in court ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2025/08/the-cpsc-and-amazon-navigating-a-shifting-regulatory-landscape/)). The Ninth Circuit held in *Planet Green* (March 2025) that Section 230 does not cover physical distribution ([Goldman](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=27539)) (moderate on the trend). This creates a tension with the first force: the logistics layer that earns the margin also brings retailer-like legal exposure. Other live matters: Booking booked a **$900m** revenue reduction over an expected FTC complaint at Priceline ([Booking 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000045/bkng-20260930.htm)), and the FTC's monopolisation trial against Amazon is set for 29 March 2027 ([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/antitrust/articles/2536525)).

**China and India.** China is moving in the opposite direction. Its platform pricing rules, effective **10 April 2026**, ban platforms from forcing merchants to sell below cost, demanding price parity or imposing automatic price-matching. Fee changes need seven days' consultation with merchants ([Allen & Gledhill](https://www.allenandgledhill.com/cn/publication/articles/33078/s-new-internet-platform-pricing-rules-key-compliance-requirements-for-platforms-and-merchants)). Western regulators restrain platforms from raising what they charge; China restrains them from pushing prices down. India's e-commerce amendment rules take effect on 1 January 2027 ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/india-ecommerce-amendment-rules-2026-key-changes-47308.html/)). They require dark-pattern self-audits, a "prior price" equal to the lowest of the past 30 days, and disclosure of ranking parameters.

### AI is hollowing out commoditised services marketplaces

The clearest measured damage from generative AI is to online freelance and lead-generation marketplaces. In Q3 2026 Fiverr's annual active buyers fell from 3.3m to **2.4m (−25.8%)** and its marketplace revenue fell 24.7%, while spend per buyer rose 12.9%; management cites "persistent search engine traffic headwinds" ([Fiverr](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1762301/000117891326004897/exhibit_99-1.htm)). Upwork's gross services volume fell **10.1%** and active clients 10%, yet its adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 34.4% through cost cuts; its bets are AI-skilled talent (volume at a run-rate of nearly $450m, up 55%) and an MCP server, launched in August 2026, through which AI agents had made nearly 50m requests ([Upwork](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1627475/000162747526000053/upwork3q26-pressrelease_3q.htm)). Angi, the US home-services lead-generation business, lost revenue (−9%) and active professionals (−14%) and cut about 300 jobs ([Angi](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1705110/000170511026000104/q32026earningsrelease.htm)) (all company-reported; high).

The academic evidence points the same way. Within eight months of ChatGPT's release, job posts for automation-prone writing and coding fell **21%** relative to manual-intensive work, and image posts fell 17% after image generators arrived. The jobs that remained were more complex and better paid ([Demirci, Hannane & Zhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4602944)). Hui, Reshef and Zhou found falls in both employment and earnings for exposed freelancers ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4527336)). The 2026 company data fit that pattern: fewer buyers, larger and more complex jobs, and higher take rates to offset volume.

It is **not known** how much of the decline comes from AI substituting for the work, how much from AI search diverting the organic Google traffic these platforms relied on, and how much from the macroeconomy.

The counter-thesis comes from investors. Olivia Moore at a16z argues that AI is reviving "graveyard" service categories, where marketplaces stalled on high customer-acquisition cost or low lifetime value, by cutting the human coordination cost of managed models. Her examples include the UK flat-fee legal platform Lawhive and an AI-native brokerage charging 1.5% against a 6% norm ([a16z](https://a16z.com/marketplaces-in-the-age-of-ai-take-two-graveyard-to-greenfield/)). Speedinvest puts it as "own the work, not just the transaction" ([Speedinvest](https://www.speedinvest.com/knowledge/own-the-work-not-just-the-transaction-why-were-still-bullish-on-marketplaces)). Both are investor essays with portfolio interests, and no outcome data yet support them. Treat the revival as a hypothesis (low–moderate).

## Services, vertical, B2B and software marketplaces are diverging

### Delivery and mobility turned profitable and are now merging

US-listed delivery and mobility platforms have reached normal profitability, with adjusted EBITDA of **3–5% of gross volume**. Uber earned $3.0bn in Q3 2026, 5.0% of $59.8bn of gross bookings that grew 20%, with $2.5bn of quarterly free cash flow ([Uber](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000039/uberq326earningspressrelea.htm)). DoorDash earned 3.1% of gross order value in Q3 2026 ([DoorDash](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1792789/000179278926000062/proddashex991-pressrelease.htm)), Lyft 3.2% of gross bookings in Q2 2026 ([Lyft](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1759509/000162828026054305/lyft-20260630xpressrelease.htm)) and Instacart 3.2% of transaction value in Q3 2026 ([Instacart](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1579091/000119312526437014/d24470dex991.htm)) (all company-reported; high). The years-of-losses story is over for the US-listed leaders; the remaining losses were in China, and they narrowed in 2026. Uber's reported revenue understates its business: revenue grew only 10%, and UK model changes cut it by **$1.1bn** in the quarter, coinciding with the VAT change described above ([Uber 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1543151/000154315126000041/uber-20260930.htm)).

Robotaxis are commercial but small. Uber expects about **700 fully driverless vehicles** on its platform by the end of 2026 and more than 2,000 in 2027 (company-reported), against 4.1bn trips in Q3 alone. Waymo reported more than 400,000 paid rides a week in February 2026 and planned launches including London ([eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/waymo-16-billion-robotaxi-expansion-2026/)); whether London service has started is not known. Investors nonetheless treat autonomy as a threat: Uber's shares hit a 52-week low on 28 October 2026 on "robotaxi fears" ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/uber-stock-hits-fresh-52-week-low-ahead-of-earnings-as-robotaxi-fears-linger/cZU9V5OR9GK)) (moderate). Uber and Lyft are positioning as aggregators of third-party autonomous fleets, and Uber has said it will spend more than $10bn on robotaxis "over the coming years" ([BNN Bloomberg/Reuters](https://beta.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/08/05/uber-forecasts-weak-quarterly-profit-doubles-down-on-robotaxi-investment-plans/)). Whether fleet owners will accept being aggregated or go direct is not known.

### Travel platforms earn the highest margins, under registration-and-data regulation

Travel marketplaces carry no labour and little logistics, and their margins show it. Booking earned a **47%** adjusted EBITDA margin in Q3 2026 on gross bookings up 10%, though it guides Q4 room nights at just +1–3%, citing Middle East disruption ([Booking](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1075531/000107553126000044/q3-26bkngearningsrelease.htm)). Airbnb earned 35% in Q2 2026 on gross booking value up 16% ([Airbnb](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001559720/000119312526337928/d70413dex991.htm)). Regulation of short-term rentals is moving from city bans to registration plus data-sharing. The EU's short-term-rental data regulation applied from 20 May 2026 ([bnbnews](https://bnbnews.gr/en/legislation/38542/airbnb-ready-for-the-new-eu-short-term-rental-regulation-are-member-states/)). Spain's national registry led Airbnb to remove nearly **65,000** listings in 2025 ([Catalan News](https://www.catalannews.com/housing-crisis/item/airbnb-removes-almost-65000-illegal-listings-at-request-of-spains-consumer-affairs-ministry)). England's registration scheme has slipped to **March 2027** ([Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2026/10/england-s-short-let-registration-scheme-what-the-m)).

### Labour law is converging on presumptions of worker status

The **EU Platform Work Directive** must be transposed by **2 December 2026**. A commercial tracker found that as of 3 November only Slovakia had adopted a dedicated law, and France's labour minister has conceded the deadline will be missed ([Teamed](https://www.teamed.global/eu-platform-work/transposition-tracker)) (moderate).

**Britain** did not create a single worker status in the Employment Rights Act 2025. On **14 October 2026** it consulted, until 8 January 2027, on a statutory **presumption of worker status** with "less emphasis on personal service" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/makework-pay-employment-status-reform-and-protections-for-the-self-employed)). That would weaken the substitution-clause defence on which UK platform contractor models rely (moderate; legislation 2027–28 at the earliest).

The **US** has settled on contractor status plus earnings floors and, in Massachusetts and California, sectoral bargaining. Lyft agreed in September 2026 to pay **$272.5m** over legacy misclassification in California ([Lyft 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1759509/000162828026064095/lyft-20260930.htm)).

### B2B is marketplace-ising at the edges, not the core

B2B marketplaces are growing fast from small bases. Amazon Business reports **$60bn** of annualised gross sales (Q2 2026) across more than 11m organisations, though the accompanying claim that sales "doubled" in six months is doubtful (low) ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/amazon-business-gains-1-8-million-organizations-as-sales-double-to-60bn/)). Faire expected nearly $3bn of 2025 GMV, with Europe growing about twice as fast as North America ([Faire](https://www.faire.com/news/2025-11-17-a-milestone-for-faire)). Mirakl, which runs marketplaces for retailers, had its first full year of profitability in 2025 on $218m of annual recurring revenue ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/02/27/mirakl-profitable-recurring-revenue-marketplace-2025/amp/)). Together these are well under 1% of a US manufacturing and wholesale economy of **$15.1tn** that grew 0.4% in 2025 ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/01/26/us-b2b-sales-exceed-15-trillion-2025/amp/)). This is an indicative comparison only. Distributors are becoming marketplace operators through tools like Mirakl rather than being displaced. Freight marketplaces remain cyclical and thin: Uber Freight only reached breakeven adjusted EBITDA in Q4 2025 ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/uber-freight-posts-flat-q4-results-as-broader-platform-posts-record-profits)) (moderate overall).

### UK portals keep 70% margins while investors price in AI risk

**Auto Trader's** core marketplace earned a **70%** operating margin in the year to March 2026 (63% for the group), but revenue grew only 4%, and it was flat in April. Its growth now comes from online transactions: deals with full reservation rose from 49,000 to 137,000 ([Auto Trader](https://www.stockopedia.com/share-prices/autotrader-LON:AUTO/news/autotrader-grp-full-year-results-for-the-year-ended-31-march-2026-019e6dd5-0ee5-77c4-8a37-bd3d575579bb/)).

**Rightmove's** H1 2026 revenue rose 7%, but underlying operating profit rose only 3% as AI spending increased. Its shares were down **42%** year to date by the end of July ([The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/rightmove-reports-h1-revenue-up-7-with-major-ai-progress/)). Two confounders make the share fall hard to attribute to AI: a £1.5bn legal claim over estate-agent fees and the overhang from REA Group's earlier takeover approach. Investors are pricing a threat that has not yet appeared in revenue (moderate).

In the US the inventory-owning transactional model now works at scale: Carvana's Q3 2026 retail units rose 39%, with adjusted EBITDA of $800m ([Carvana](https://finviz.com/news/410551/carvana-announces-record-third-quarter-2026-results)).

On ticketing, the UK's pledged resale cap was shelved in April 2026 and is not expected before the 2027–28 parliamentary session ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/04/30/uk-ticket-scalping-ban-shelved-2026/)). In the US, a jury found Live Nation–Ticketmaster liable under antitrust law in April 2026 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/15/live-nation-ticketmaster-lose-antitrust-case)).

### Recommerce is the healthiest product category, and it is consolidating

Beyond Vinted and eBay–Depop, luxury consignment is showing operating leverage while mid-market managed resale is not. The RealReal grew GMV 23% in Q3 2026 and raised its full-year guidance ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/business/the-realreal-raises-full-year-guidance-as-third-quarter-revenue-grows-19-percent/2026103075479)), and Vestiaire Collective targets its first annual profit in 2026 ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.uk/news/business/vestiaire-collective-eyes-profitability-in-2026-ahead-of-us-expansion-push/2026020586149)). ThredUp, by contrast, guided Q4 2026 revenue **2% lower** year on year ([ThredUp](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001484778/000148477826000031/exhibit991earningsreleaseq.htm)). Market-size figures are weak. ThredUp's resale report is produced by a resale operator with its paid analyst partner, is survey-based and is inconsistent on whether its $393bn global figure is current or for 2030 ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260402799816/en/ThredUps-14th-Annual-Resale-Report-Reveals-New-Era-of-Structural-Competition-and-AI-Driven-Discovery)) (low). No independent measure of UK recommerce GMV exists.

### App-store tolls are eroding through steering rules, not rival stores

In the US, Apple has been barred since April 2025 from charging commission on purchases made through outside links. In December 2025 the Ninth Circuit allowed a "reasonable" fee and sent the question back; in April 2026 it lifted a stay of that ruling ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/04/29/epic-games-wins-reversal-app-store-fee-battle/)). The Supreme Court separately granted Apple certiorari on 30 June 2026 ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/06/30/high-court-grants-cert-in-apples-challenge-to-ninth-circuit-contempt-ruling-in-app-store-dispute/)).

Google's November 2025 settlement with Epic proposed fees of 10–20% ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Google-cuts-Play-Store-fees-as-Epic-settlement-reshapes-Android-app-payments.1243278.0.html)). Later secondary reporting says it was withdrawn on 15 July 2026, leaving the 2024 injunction in force ([Open Class Actions](https://openclassactions.com/news/epic-google-play-store-settlement-withdrawn.php)) (moderate; see conflicts). The EU fined Google **€430m** for Play anti-steering in July 2026 ([European Commission](https://digital-markets-act.ec.europa.eu/commission-fines-google-eur890-million-breaches-digital-markets-act-2026-07-23_en)).

Rival iOS stores in the EU remain marginal; Setapp Mobile closed in February 2026 ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/01/23/apple-blames-ec-blocking-app-store-fee-changes/)). The UK CMA has secured only procedural commitments so far (from April 2026) and is still consulting on steering ([CMA](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-cmas-programme-of-work-across-mobile-platforms)).

The growth in software marketplaces is in agents, though there is little evidence yet of money changing hands. AWS Marketplace had more than 3,400 AI-agent listings by July 2026; when the category opened in July 2025 AWS had expected about 50 partners and got 900 ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/07/15/aws-product-listing-service-enterprise-agents-ai-awsmarketplaceseries/)) (company statement; no global GMV published). ChatGPT's app directory has no disclosed commission ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-launches-app-submissions-and-rolls-out-store-in-the-new-year/)), and the MCP Registry is a free directory that listed about 30,000 servers by September 2026 on one developer's count ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/amareswer/the-mcp-registry-by-the-numbers-38nc)) (low). These are distribution channels first and toll booths later, which is why their significance for incumbents today lies in disintermediation risk rather than revenue.

## Physical markets, exchanges and prediction markets show the alternatives

**Britain's physical markets.** The number of traditional retail markets has stayed flat at about **1,150** for fifteen years, but their health has worsened. Stall occupancy fell from 78% in 2018 to **72% in 2021/22**, only 48% of markets ran a surplus, and over 80% of traders are aged 41 or over ([GLA, presenting NABMA data](https://www.london.gov.uk/media/100312/download)) (moderate; no newer national survey was found).

The markets that thrive have been reinvested in as destinations. Leeds Kirkgate drew 5.9m visits in 2024, up 33% since 2021, alongside a £10m refurbishment ([Leeds City Council](https://news.leeds.gov.uk/news/leeds-kirkgate-market-sets-stall-out-for-another-big-year-after-bumper-2024)). Food halls are pruning rather than collapsing: Boxpark abandoned its Birmingham and Bristol sites in February 2026 ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/uk/news/boxpark-has-officially-abandoned-plans-to-open-new-venues-in-two-uk-cities-022026)).

Smithfield and Billingsgate are due to close in 2028, after the City of London Corporation abandoned the Dagenham relocation in November 2024, having spent £308m on it ([Building Design](https://www.bdonline.co.uk/news/futures-of-smithfield-and-billingsgate-markets-uncertain-as-city-of-london-corporation-pulls-plug-on-dagenham-dock-site/5133019.article)). Closing them requires a private Act of Parliament, which was carried over into the 2026–27 session ([UK Parliament](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/3896)). A platform can close a marketplace by changing its terms; a chartered market needs Parliament.

**Informal markets.** Globally, informality still dominates. About **58%** of workers are informal ([People Matters, citing ILO](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/economy-policy/nearly-58percent-of-global-workforce-still-trapped-in-informal-employment-ilo-report-49690)). Neighbourhood kirana stores held about **91%** of Indian grocery in 2025 ([RedSeer via NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/indias-mass-grocery-continues-rely-kiranas-report-467.htm)) (moderate). Venture-funded B2B apps for informal retailers have retrenched towards payments and credit, as at MaxAB–Wasoko ([Techeconomy](https://techeconomy.ng/wasoko-cofounder-daniel-yu-steps-down-india-malengo/)). Reports that Twiga Foods entered administration rest on headlines only and are unverified.

**Exchanges.** Exchange groups are becoming data utilities. ICE's 2025 revenue was **51%** recurring ([ICE](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2026/Intercontinental-Exchange-Reports-Strong-Full-Year-2025-Results/default.aspx)), and Data & Analytics was about 44% of LSEG's income ([LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/lseg/en_us/documents/investor-relations/financial-results/preliminary-results/rns/lseg-2025-preliminary-results-rns-26feb2026.pdf)) (high). Nasdaq and NYSE Arca target overnight trading from **6 December 2026** ([Harvard Law School Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/10/21/nyse-arca-and-nasdaq-to-launch-extended-trading-what-companies-should-consider/)), and LSE plans "LSE 24" for H1 2027. London's listed-company count fell from about 2,365 in 2015 to about 1,560 in 2025 ([Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.ilsole24ore.com/art/exodus-from-the-london-stock-exchange-800-listed-companies-lost-over-ten-years-AIOPbadD)) (moderate).

**Prediction markets.** These are the fastest-growing marketplaces covered here, and the least clear about what they are. Kalshi's October 2026 volume was about **$65bn** by one count and **$97.8bn** by another; the difference is whether each leg of a multi-leg "combo" bet is counted at $1 face value. Dollars actually paid in are only about 20–30% of contract volume, but even on the lower count growth from about $4.4bn in October 2025 is unambiguous ([KuCoin, citing The Block](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/kalshi-and-polymarket-us-set-new-trading-volume-records-ahead-of-midterm-elections); [DeFiRate](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/volume/kalshi/)). Kalshi's valuation rose from $11bn (December 2025) to $22bn (May 2026), with talks reported at $40bn, and nearly **90%** of its 2025 revenue came from sports ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/kalshi-and-polymarket-just-posted-their-biggest-trading-month-on-record/); [Crypto News Australia](https://cryptonews.com.au/news/kalshi-and-polymarket-each-eye-20b-valuations-as-prediction-markets-hit-18b-monthly-volume-133170)) (press reports; moderate). Functionally, this is less a "marketplace of beliefs" than a federally regulated route into sports wagering. With 149 legal matters open and the federal appeals courts split, the Third Circuit backing exclusive CFTC jurisdiction and the Ninth and Sixth backing the states ([DeFiRate tracker](https://defirate.com/prediction-markets/legal/)), whether it becomes a national market or a patchwork of state gambling regimes is heading for the Supreme Court. On accuracy, experts disagree but the positions are reconcilable: a PNAS study of about a million contracts finds good aggregate calibration ([Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/10/prediction-markets-are-accurate.html)), while a Vanderbilt study finds next-day price reversals and influence from large traders in individual Polymarket markets ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/markets/polymarket-kalshi-prediction-markets-not-so-reliable-says-study)). Incumbent exchanges are buying in rather than fighting; ICE agreed in October 2025 to invest up to $2bn in Polymarket ([Startup Fortune](https://startupfortune.com/kalshi-and-polymarket-just-posted-their-biggest-trading-month-on-record/)).

**Who sets the rules.** Across these forms there are three governance models. Council and wholesale markets rest on statute. Exchange rule changes need regulator approval. Platforms set fees unilaterally and are policed afterwards. That difference explains why physical markets change slowly and platforms pivot fast (moderate; my synthesis).

## The six historical patterns still hold, with two twists

| Pattern from the history | 2026 evidence | Verdict | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Franchise and toll**: an authority grants the right to trade and takes a cut | Tolls rising via ads (Etsy 24.2%→26.2%; Amazon all-in ~39–50%+). The franchise is now conferred after the fact by designation (very large online platform, gatekeeper, strategic market status), upheld by the General Court. China now regulates fee changes | Holds and strengthening; the toll has moved off the price list | High |
| **Engineered liquidity**: thickness is manufactured in time and space | Subsidy wars (Taobao's RMB50bn; Meituan's swing of about RMB59bn into loss), free-shipping thresholds (MercadoLibre), mergers that pool couriers and shoppers (DoorDash–Instacart) | Holds; capital has replaced the legal catchment. New: regulators cap the subsidies (China) | Moderate–high |
| **Hybrid trust**: reputation works inside public enforcement | DSA fines, PRMA proposals, CPSC "distributor" ruling. AI-written reviews carry "Verified Purchase" badges (vendor estimate ~3% on top Amazon products, [Pangram](https://www.pangram.com/blog/ai-amazon-reviews)). Visa and Google build agent-authentication protocols | Holds and intensifies. Twist: trust infrastructure may migrate to payment rails | High (pattern); low–moderate (migration) |
| **Forestalling to anti-steering inversion**: medieval law protected the market from off-market deals; modern law protects off-market deals from the market | Google €430m Play anti-steering fine; Apple barred from link-out commission; CMA steering consultation. But Amazon and eBay block outside agents, a private anti-forestalling rule, and the Ninth Circuit so far treats the agent as the user's tool | Holds for app stores. **Being rewritten** by agents: whether law protects the "forestaller" agent is the next contest | Moderate |
| **Place versus process**: abstraction advances, physical markets find new jobs | Council markets weaken; Kirkgate and food halls survive as experience; exchanges become data utilities. Twist: digital marketplaces are acquiring physical place, through local warehouses forced by duty changes, PUDO networks and dark stores | Holds, with a **reversal at the logistics layer** | Moderate |
| **Closure and opening**: incumbents close markets; rulers open them when it pays | Openings: DMA, Epic, the Perplexity ruling. Closures: agent blocking and delivery mergers. Rulers also closed loopholes when it paid (de minimis), and China restrained competition to protect merchants | Holds, but "when it pays" now cuts both ways | Moderate |

The parallels that held best in the long record (franchise, toll and publicly backed trust) are the ones most strengthened by 2025–26 events. The new element is that **governments now intervene against excessive competition as well as against monopoly**. China's anti-"involution" rules are the clearest case. The closest historical analogue is guild and charter protection against "ruinous" undercutting, a parallel I have not tested against the history (low).

## Implications for anyone building or investing in a marketplace

### For someone building a marketplace

**Own the work, not the listing.** The evidence of 2025–26 runs against pure matching: freelance and lead-generation volumes are falling, portals are spending to stay visible inside AI assistants, and agent and AI-search interfaces bypass the listing page. The positions that hold up carry fulfilment, payment, guarantees or liability, because liability cannot be automated away. But the CPSC ruling and *Planet Green* show that fulfilment also carries legal exposure, so budget for compliance as product cost, not overhead.

**Plan the monetisation stack early, but do not count on retail media at sub-scale.** Ads and logistics are where margin comes from, yet WARC's forecast of 9.8% growth outside Amazon, and Amazon's 78% share of US retail media, mean a small marketplace's ad business will be modest. Ad-driven take-rate rises are also starting to look to regulators like hidden fees, as the CMA's drip-pricing cases, India's ban on bundled fees and China's seven-day notice rule for fee changes all show.

**Build for agents without handing them checkout.** Expose structured catalogues and MCP endpoints, as Auto Trader and Upwork have done. Keep merchant-of-record status. Walmart's single data point (click-out converting three times better than in-chat checkout) is the best evidence available.

**Build in Britain to the EU's standard now.** The UK's product-safety proposals (know-your-business checks, pre-listing assurance for high-risk goods, a possible UK responsible person for overseas sellers) largely mirror EU duties already in force. The £135 relief ends by March 2029 at the latest, HMRC seller reporting is live, a presumption of worker status is under consultation for services marketplaces, and England's short-let registration begins in March 2027. A builder who designs to the EU standard loses little and avoids a retrofit.

**Pick a category where trust or excitement substitutes for scale.** Live collectibles and luxury consignment show this works. Do not enter UK C2C fashion head-on against a platform holding 50–60% of GMV.

### For someone investing in one

**Value ads and fulfilment as separate businesses.** The gap between ad growth and GMV growth is the runway: Shopee's 65.6% against 28.4%, or MercadoLibre at 2.6% of GMV against Amazon's ~7%. But take-rate expansion is a lever that eventually runs out, and sellers on Amazon already lose 39–50%+ of revenue to the platform.

**Underwrite exits through strategic sale or secondary sale, not IPO.** The 2025–26 liquidity came from takeovers and secondaries. StubHub closed 6.4% below its IPO price on its first day of trading in September 2025 ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/stubhub-retail-investors-unfazed-after-stock-drops-on-nyse-debut/chDcZkrR3GI)). Faire's 58% markdown is a reminder that the base rate for marketplaces is failure or repricing; the playbook is built from survivors.

**Treat policy as a first-order risk factor, not a footnote.** A single customs change cut Shein's US revenue by double digits. Worker-status rules, DSA fines and VAT reinterpretation (Uber's £2.6bn) now move the profit and loss account.

**Be sceptical of both the AI panic and the AI pitch.** Rightmove's 42% share fall prices a threat not yet visible in revenue. The "AI revives graveyard categories" thesis comes from funds raising money on it. Ask for cohort data on traffic sources and agent-originated demand.

## Conclusion

The history of marketplaces suggested that governance and the toll matter more than technology, and 2026 confirms it more sharply than expected: regulation that deputises the operator, customs rules that strip out exemptions, antitrust that tolerates delivery mega-mergers and AI interfaces competing to sit in front of the buyer all act on the toll and the franchise rather than the matching engine. The surprise is how far marketplaces have become logistics-and-media businesses with a matching function attached. Profit has moved down the stack into warehouses, ad auctions and payments, while matching becomes a commodity that AI assistants will increasingly perform for free. That leaves operators exposed on two flanks at once: regulators are attaching liability to the physical layer that now earns the margin, and agents threaten the attention layer that funds the ads.

Three judgements follow, each with moderate confidence. The next decisive contest is legal and contractual, not technical: whether the buyer's agent is treated as the buyer, which would make it a lawful forestaller, or as a trespasser. Britain will spend 2027–28 importing EU-style marketplace duties on product safety, customs and worker status. And the marketplaces that keep their tolls will be those that charge for certainty rather than introductions (guaranteed delivery, guaranteed authenticity, guaranteed recourse), because an agent can supply the introduction for free but cannot stand behind the outcome.
